The chip is neutral until it concerns you. Category drives only the icon tint. The fill, border and label stay warm neutral until conflicting is set, at which point the whole chip takes the caution palette — 10% fill, 20% border, full-strength text. A screen full of red chips means the dish clashes with the diner's profile, not that it contains allergens.
Two conflict treatments, deliberately different. A canonical tag tints its icon and keeps the word neutral. A custom avoid-term the diner typed themselves tints the word and has no icon. A dietMismatch tag strikes the label through.
Radius is 12, not a pill. Easy to get wrong — the buttons beside them are also 12, but the visual language of a chip usually implies a capsule. It is a rounded rectangle here.
The sparkle marks an AI-inferred tag, not one printed on the menu. Provenance is never silently dropped: if a tag has no glyph to badge, the sparkle renders inline instead. There is exactly one filled style regardless of provenance — an earlier outlined-vs-filled treatment was retired in July 2026 for confusing people.
The label colour is an accessibility fix, not a preference. #6B5747 replaced #8B7364, which measured 4.08:1 on the chip fill and failed AA. The current value clears 6.3:1.
